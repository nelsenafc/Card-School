Before Wizards

Where the cards come from

Every set is downstream of a video game

Cards never invent Pokemon. A Game Boy game does, and the cards follow one to two years later. Three games cover this whole story, and each one is set in a different region: a named map with its own roster of creatures.

Wizards never printed a single Hoenn card. Generation 3 arrived and the licence went to Pokemon USA in the same season.
The clean version. Wizards of the Coast covered generations 1 and 2 and stopped. If a Pokemon is from Kanto or Johto, a Wizards card of it exists. If it is from Hoenn or later, one never will.

What the cartoon and the films did

The games created the Pokemon. The screen created the demand, which is why so many cards were printed.

April 1997The anime startsJapan first, then the United States in September 1998. It turned a Game Boy game into a mass audience.
1999 to 2000The demand spikeWizards printed Base Set in the millions to keep up. That Unlimited run is why common vintage cards stay cheap.
2000Films made their own cardsAncient Mew came free with a cinema ticket to Pokemon the Movie 2000. Much of the promo line works this way.

What the top card actually sold for

Mar 2022
$420,000
Dec 2025
$550,000

Both are public auction results for a gem-mint graded 1st Edition Base Set Charizard. The 2025 sale is the record for a Wizards-era card.

Not the overall record. A 1998 Japanese Pikachu Illustrator sold for $16.49M in February 2026, roughly thirty times the Charizard. The top of the Pokemon market is Japanese and predates Wizards entirely.
The break

How it ended

Nintendo built its own American publisher and stopped needing an outside one.

Jan 1999LicenceWizards launches Base Set in English.
Sep 1999Hasbro buys WizardsPokemon is the crown jewel.
2002Relationship soursPokemon USA is standing up its own operation.
May 2003SkyridgeThe last set Wizards ever printed.
Jul 2003HandoverEX Ruby and Sapphire ships under Pokemon USA.
Oct 2003LawsuitWizards sues Nintendo in Seattle. The licence never returns.
Why the date matters. Nothing printed after Skyridge has a 1st Edition stamp. The frame, the foil and the printer all changed. "Vintage" in English Pokemon means "printed by Wizards".
Cheat sheet

Terms

The Pokemon world

GenerationA wave of new games and the creatures they add. This era covers gens 1 to 3.
KantoThe gen 1 region. The original 151, from Red and Green.
JohtoThe gen 2 region. 100 more, from Gold and Silver.
HoennThe gen 3 region. Wizards never printed it.
Gym LeaderA boss character in the games. Brock, Misty, Blaine, Giovanni.
EeveelutionsThe forms Eevee evolves into. Jungle gave the first three.
UnownA Pokemon shaped like a letter. 26 of them, one per letter.
PromoA card given away rather than sold in packs. Films, magazines, events.

Print and rarity

1st EditionStamp marking a set's first print run. WOTC era only.
ShadowlessNo stamp, no drop shadow. The second Base Set run.
UnlimitedDrop shadow, heavier type, printed in bulk.
HoloFoil in the artwork window.
Reverse holoFoil on the card body instead.
Secret rareNumbered above the set total.
CentringBorder evenness. Decides most grades.
SlabThe graded plastic case, with a cert number.
No RarityThe 1996 Japanese first print. Japan's 1st Edition.
e-ReaderA Game Boy accessory that scanned the dot codes on Expedition-era cards.
